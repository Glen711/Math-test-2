Module 1 • 3-Minute Intuition

1. The Big Picture: What Are Limits & Derivatives Actually Doing?

Before we touch a single scary symbol, picture two everyday situations from your NCERT textbook:

  • The Falling Stone (Instantaneous Speed): Imagine dropping a stone off a tall cliff. Its distance changes every split second according to $s = 4.9t^2$. You don't want its average speed over an hour—you want the exact speedometer reading at $t = 2\text{ s}$.
  • The Car & The Pothole (Limits): Picture a car driving toward a speed bump at the $2\text{ m}$ mark. Even if a huge pothole sits right at $2\text{ m}$ so the car can't stop on it, watching where the road leads from just before $2\text{ m}$ ($1.99\text{ m}$) and just after $2\text{ m}$ ($2.01\text{ m}$) tells you its exact expected position.
📘 Exam-Ready Big Picture Calculus is the branch of mathematics that studies how the value of a function changes as points in its domain change.
  • Limits tell us what value a function approaches near a point—even when direct substitution gives $\frac{0}{0}$.
  • Derivatives use limits to measure the exact instantaneous rate of change—visually, the slope of the tangent to a curve at a single point.

Module 2 • Concept 2.1

2. Intuitive Idea of Limits, LHL & RHL

Why can't we always just plug $x = a$ directly into $f(x)$? Because at $x = a$, the function might blow up into $\frac{0}{0}$ (like $\frac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2}$ at $x = 2$) or have a sudden cliff-like jump. Checking values just to the left and just to the right reveals where the graph is truly heading.

📘 Exam-Ready Definitions
  • Left Hand Limit (LHL): The expected value of $f$ at $x = a$ given values of $f$ near $x$ to the left of $a$, written $\lim_{x \to a^-} f(x)$.
  • Right Hand Limit (RHL): The expected value of $f$ at $x = a$ given values of $f$ near $x$ to the right of $a$, written $\lim_{x \to a^+} f(x)$.
  • Existence of Limit: If $\text{LHL} = \text{RHL}$, that common value is the limit $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$. If $\text{LHL} \ne \text{RHL}$, the limit does not exist.
  • Crucial Exam Note: $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$ and $f(a)$ may not be the same!

📝 Problem-Solving Template (Piecewise / Modulus Functions)
  1. Step 1 (LHL): Pick the branch defined for $x < a$ and plug in $x = a$.
  2. Step 2 (RHL): Pick the branch defined for $x > a$ and plug in $x = a$.
  3. Step 3 (Compare): If $\text{LHL} = \text{RHL}$, write "Limit exists and equals ___." If not, write "$\text{LHL} \ne \text{RHL}$, so the limit does not exist."
Module 3 • Concept 2.2

3. Algebra of Limits, The $\frac{0}{0}$ Funnel & Theorem 2

Good news! For any normal polynomial like $x^3 - x^2 + 1$, limits are super easy: just plug in $x = a$ directly! $$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$$ Limits also split cleanly across addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division (as long as the denominator doesn't become $0$).

🚨 What If Direct Substitution Gives $\frac{0}{0}$? Hear an alarm bell! Never write $\frac{0}{0} = 0$ or $1$. Getting $\frac{0}{0}$ simply means the hidden "troublemaker factor" $(x - a)$ is sitting in both the numerator and denominator. Factor it out, cancel it, and plug $x = a$ in again!
🧪 Interactive Lab 2: The $\frac{0}{0}$ Cancellation Funnel Click "Next Step" to watch how every $\frac{0}{0}$ problem collapses!

Power Limit Shortcut (Theorem 2) — High-Yield Formula + 5-Mark Proof

📘 Theorem 2 Formula $$\lim_{x \to a} \frac{x^n - a^n}{x - a} = n a^{n-1}$$ Valid for any positive integer $n$ (and also rational $n$ when $a > 0$).
⚡ Quick Practice Example Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^5 - 32}{x - 2}$:
Rewrite $32 = 2^5$, so we have $\lim_{x \to 2} \frac{x^5 - 2^5}{x - 2}$. Here $n = 5$ and $a = 2$. Plug into $n a^{n-1}$: $$5 \times 2^{5-1} = 5 \times 2^4 = 5 \times 16 = \mathbf{80}$$

4. Sandwich Theorem & Trigonometric Limits

Why can't we use our factoring trick on $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x}$? Because plugging in $x = 0$ gives $\frac{0}{0}$, and you cannot factor $\sin x$ like a polynomial! Instead, we trap $\frac{\sin x}{x}$ between two "slices of bread" that both squeeze down to $1$.

📘 Theorem 4 (Sandwich Theorem) & Theorem 5 (Standard Trig Limits)

Sandwich Theorem: If $f(x) \le g(x) \le h(x)$ and $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = l = \lim_{x \to a} h(x)$, then $\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = l$.

Two Golden Trig Limits ($x$ MUST be in radians): $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1 \qquad \text{and} \qquad \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x} = 0$$

🧪 Interactive Lab 3: Unit Circle Proof Explorer ($\cos x < \frac{\sin x}{x} < 1$) Drag angle $x$ toward $0$ and watch the sandwich squeeze!

3 Nested Areas (Unit Circle, Radius $OA = 1$):

Small $\triangle OAC$ ($\frac{1}{2}\sin x$)—
Sector $OAC$ ($\frac{1}{2}x$)—
Big $\triangle OAB$ ($\frac{1}{2}\tan x$)—
Middle $\frac{\sin x}{x}$—

Notice: As you slide $x \to 0.05$, $\cos x$ climbs right up to $1$, trapping $\frac{\sin x}{x}$ at $1$!

The 3 Must-Know Trig Limit Tricks (Easy 1–2 Marks!)

  • 1. "Match the Box" Trick ($\frac{\sin(kx)}{x}$): Whatever sits inside $\sin(\text{box})$ must sit right underneath it! Example: $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 4x}{\sin 2x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 4x}{4x} \cdot \frac{2x}{\sin 2x} \cdot \frac{4x}{2x} = 1 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 = \mathbf{2}$.
  • 2. $\tan x$ Limit: Always rewrite $\tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x}$: $$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\tan x}{x} = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} \cdot \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{\cos x} = 1 \cdot \frac{1}{1} = \mathbf{1}$$
  • 3. $1 - \cos x$ Limit: Remember the half-angle identity $1 - \cos x = 2\sin^2\!\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)$!

5. Derivatives: Geometrical Meaning & First Principle

Why does the First Principle formula have $\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$? Take two points on a curve: $P(a, f(a))$ and $Q(a+h, f(a+h))$. The fraction $\frac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h}$ is just "Rise over Run"—the slope of the secant chord $PQ$. As $h \to 0$, point $Q$ slides straight onto point $P$, turning the chord into the tangent line at $P$! Geometrically, $f'(a) = \tan\psi$, where $\psi$ is the angle the tangent makes with the positive $x$-axis.

📘 Exam-Ready Definition (First Principle of Derivative) $$f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$$ Whenever an exam question says "find the derivative from first principle", you MUST start with this exact formula!
🧪 Interactive Lab 4: Secant-to-Tangent Derivative Machine ($f(x) = x^2$) Shrink the gap $h \to 0$ and watch the slope lock onto $2x$!

🎯 4-Step Mission for Every First Principle Question ("Cancel the Bottom $h$!") Worked Example: Find the derivative of $f(x) = x^2$ from first principle.
  1. Step 1 (Write Formula — earns 1 mark!): $f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$
  2. Step 2 (Substitute & Expand): $\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(x+h)^2 - x^2}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{x^2 + 2xh + h^2 - x^2}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{2xh + h^2}{h}$
  3. Step 3 (Factor $h$ & Cancel): $\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{h(2x + h)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} (2x + h)$
  4. Step 4 (Plug in $h = 0$): $2x + 0 = \mathbf{2x}$!

6. Fast Differentiation Rules: Power, Product, Quotient & Trig

Whenever a question simply says "Find the derivative of..." (without saying "from first principle"), use these fast shortcuts!

Rule Name Formula Easy Memory Rhyme Worked Example
Constant Rule $\frac{d}{dx}(c) = 0$ "Standalone numbers vanish to 0." $\frac{d}{dx}(99) = 0$
Power Rule $\frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = n x^{n-1}$ "Drop power to front, subtract 1 from top." $\frac{d}{dx}(4x^3 + 7) = 12x^2 + 0 = 12x^2$
Sum / Difference $(u \pm v)' = u' \pm v'$ "Differentiate term-by-term." $\frac{d}{dx}(x^2 - \sin x) = 2x - \cos x$
Product Rule (Leibnitz) $(u \cdot v)' = u'v + uv'$ "Deriv of 1st × 2nd PLUS 1st × deriv of 2nd." $\frac{d}{dx}(x^2 \sin x) = (2x)\sin x + x^2(\cos x)$
Quotient Rule $\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)' = \frac{u'v - uv'}{v^2}$ "Low $d(\text{high})$ MINUS high $d(\text{low})$, all over low squared." $\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{x+1}{x}\right) = \frac{1\cdot x - (x+1)\cdot 1}{x^2} = -\frac{1}{x^2}$
💡 The "CO-" Minus Sign Trick for Trig Derivatives
  • $\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x) = \cos x$   |   $\frac{d}{dx}(\mathbf{co}\text{s } x) = \mathbf{-}\sin x$
  • $\frac{d}{dx}(\tan x) = \sec^2 x$   |   $\frac{d}{dx}(\mathbf{co}\text{t } x) = \mathbf{-}\csc^2 x$
  • $\frac{d}{dx}(\sec x) = \sec x \tan x$   |   $\frac{d}{dx}(\mathbf{co}\text{sec } x) = \mathbf{-}\csc x \cot x$
Notice the pattern? Every trig function starting with "co-" gives a negative sign ($-$) in its derivative!

8. Common 1st PUC Exam Traps (Avoid These!)

Trap 1: Evaluating $\frac{0}{0}$ directly
❌ Writing $\frac{0}{0} = 0$ or $\frac{0}{0} = 1$.
✅ Always factor out and cancel $(x - a)$ before substituting $x = a$.
Trap 2: Sign/Denominator error in Quotient Rule
❌ Writing $u'v + uv'$ or forgetting $v^2$ at the bottom.
✅ Use $\frac{u'v - uv'}{v^2}$: differentiate top $u$ first, use MINUS, square bottom $v$.
Trap 3: Not matching the angle in $\sin(kx)$
❌ Writing $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin 4x}{\sin 2x} = 1$.
✅ Rewrite as $\frac{\sin 4x}{4x} \cdot \frac{2x}{\sin 2x} \cdot 2 = 1 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 = 2$.
Trap 4: Assuming $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$
❌ Stopping just because $f(a)$ is undefined or equals a dot.
✅ Check LHL and RHL near $x = a$ independently of $f(a)$.
Trap 5: Power Rule shortcut on "First Principle"
❌ Directly writing $(x^2)' = 2x$ when asked "from first principle".
✅ Always start with $\lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$ or you lose full method marks!

🌙 5-Minute Night-Before Revision Box (Read on Dec 9 Night!)

  • Existence of Limit: $\lim_{x \to a} f(x)$ exists iff $\text{LHL} = \text{RHL}$; it does not depend on $f(a)$.
  • $\frac{0}{0}$ Rational Limits: Factor and cancel $(x - a)$, or use $\lim_{x \to a} \frac{x^n - a^n}{x - a} = n a^{n-1}$.
  • Trig Limits ($x$ in radians): $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin x}{x} = 1$ (Sandwich Theorem) and $\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1 - \cos x}{x} = 0$.
  • First Principle: $f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$, geometrically equal to the slope of the tangent ($\tan\psi$).
  • Product & Quotient Rules: $(uv)' = u'v + uv'$ and $\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)' = \frac{u'v - uv'}{v^2}$.
  • Core Derivatives: $\frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = nx^{n-1}$, $\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x) = \cos x$, $\frac{d}{dx}(\cos x) = -\sin x$, $\frac{d}{dx}(\text{constant}) = 0$.
